1. A New Economic Order
A new economic order is based on comparative cultural advantages.
- Many countries have cheap labour, but only a few have become economic powerhouses.
- Different countries develop competitive advantages through their culture, management practices and business environment.
Examples:
1. Japanese Quality
2. Korean Manufacturing
3. Indian Information Technology (IT)
4. Chinese Entrepreneurship
5. Rule-Based Cultures
6. Japanese Quality
Japanese business culture focuses on quality, teamwork and continuous improvement.

Key Features:
- Group-Oriented: Importance is given to teamwork and collective goals.
- Long-Time Horizon: Focus on long-term growth rather than short-term benefits.
- Group Harmony: Maintaining cooperation and harmony within the organisation.
- Continuous Improvement: Regular improvement in products, processes and working methods.
Superior Operations Management

Japanese organisations are known for efficient operations management.
- JIT (Just-in-Time) Inventory Management: Maintaining inventory according to actual requirements to reduce unnecessary stock.
- Kanban System: A visual management system used to control workflow and inventory.
  - Minimises rework and waste.
  - Maximises flexibility.
3. Korean Manufacturing
- Strong relationship between leading industrial families and the government.
- Close cooperation between business groups and government institutions.
- This relationship has contributed to industrial growth and manufacturing development.
4. Indian Information Technology (IT)
- India has developed a strong position in the global IT industry.
- IT services and technological expertise are important contributors to its economic development.
5. Chinese Entrepreneurship
- Entrepreneurship plays an important role in China's economic development.
- Business expansion and entrepreneurial activities contribute to economic growth.
6. Rule-Based Cultures
- These cultures emphasise rules, regulations and established procedures.
- Business decisions and activities are guided by formal standards and institutional frameworks.

BUSINESS ETHICS: CROSS-COUNTRY PERSPECTIVE
1. Theoretical Foundation of Decision Framework
Core Concepts
Ethical Relativism

Ethical relativism suggests that ethical standards may differ across cultures and societies.

It can be understood through two perspectives:

1. Cultural Imperialism (Absolutism):
- Applying one's own cultural values and ethical standards to other cultures.
- Assumes that certain standards should be followed universally.
2. Hypernorms:
- Universal basic principles based on fundamental human values.
- These principles provide common ethical standards across different cultures.
2. Introduction
- The world is moving towards a multipolar equilibrium, where multiple countries and economic powers influence global affairs.
- Different cultures have different ethical values and business practices.
- Understanding cultural ethics is important for international business.
Cultural Ethics
Cultural ethics refers to the ethical values and moral principles influenced by culture.

Important elements include:
- Integrity
- Religion
- Cultural values
Ethics can be understood through the relationship between:
- Culture
- Morality
- Principles of values
3. Branches of Ethics
There are five major branches of ethics:

4. Meta-Ethics
- Studies the meaning, nature and origin of moral concepts.
- Examines what makes something right or wrong.
2. Normative Ethics
- Deals with standards of right and wrong behaviour.
- Establishes moral principles that guide human actions.
3. Applied Ethics
- Applies ethical principles to real-life situations.
- Includes business ethics, medical ethics and environmental ethics.
4. Moral Psychology
- Studies the development and nature of moral capacity.
- Examines how people develop moral judgement and behaviour.
5. Disciplinary Ethics

- Focuses on ethical standards and responsibilities within specific disciplines or professions.
4. Cross-Cultural Issues
Cross-cultural issues arise when people from different cultural backgrounds interact in business.
A. Implications for International Marketing
- Businesses operate in different cultural environments.
- Marketing strategies must consider local values, beliefs and traditions.
- Cultural understanding helps businesses serve international markets effectively.
B. Cultural Diversity
- Different societies have different beliefs, values and traditions.
- Cultural diversity creates both opportunities and challenges for businesses.
- Organisations must respect cultural differences.
C. Cross-Cultural Challenges
- Cross-cultural issues create challenges in international markets.
- Different cultural expectations can affect business relationships.
- Companies must adapt their practices according to different cultural environments.
D. Different Ethical Standards
- A major challenge arises when two different ethical standards meet during a business transaction.
- Business organisations must understand the ethical expectations of different countries.
- Maintaining consistency in business practices across cultures is important.
