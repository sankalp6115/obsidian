### 1. Theoretical Foundation of Decision Framework

The theoretical foundation of business ethics helps us understand how ethical decisions are made across different cultures and societies.

Core Concepts

A. Ethical Relativism

- Ethical values and standards may differ from one culture to another.
- What is considered right in one society may be considered wrong in another.

It includes two important perspectives:

1\. Cultural Imperialism (Absolutism)

- Applying one's own cultural values and ethical standards to other societies.
- Belief that certain ethical principles should be followed universally.

2\. Hypernorms

- Universal basic principles based on fundamental human values.
- These principles provide common ethical standards across different cultures.

### 2. Introduction

The world is moving towards a Multipolar Equilibrium.

- Multipolar equilibrium refers to a global system in which multiple countries and powers influence international affairs.
- Different countries have different cultural values, beliefs and ethical practices.

Cultural Ethics

Cultural ethics refers to moral values and principles influenced by culture, society and traditions.

Important elements of cultural ethics:

- Values of culture
- Integrity
- Religion
- Morality
- Principles of right and wrong

Meaning of Ethics:

Ethics is the study of moral principles that guide human behaviour and help distinguish between right and wrong.

### 3. Branches of Ethics

There are five major branches of ethics:

1\. Meta-Ethics

- Studies the meaning and nature of moral values.
- Examines the origin of ethical concepts such as right, wrong, good and bad.

2\. Normative Ethics

- Deals with moral standards and rules of behaviour.
- Determines how individuals should behave.

3\. Applied Ethics

- Applies ethical principles to practical, real-life situations.
- Examples: Business ethics, medical ethics and environmental ethics.

4\. Moral Psychology

- Studies the development and nature of moral capacity.
- Explains how people develop moral judgement and ethical behaviour.

5\. Disciplinary Ethics

- Deals with ethical principles and professional responsibilities within specific disciplines.
- Ensures discipline-specific standards of conduct.

### 4. Cross-Cultural Issues

Cross-cultural issues arise when individuals or organisations from different cultural backgrounds interact with each other.

A. Implications for International Marketing

- Companies operate in different countries with different cultural values.
- Marketing strategies must be adapted according to local traditions, beliefs and preferences.
- Understanding cultural differences helps businesses succeed in international markets.

B. Cultural Diversity

- Cultural diversity refers to the existence of different cultures, traditions, beliefs and values.
- It influences consumer behaviour and business practices.
- Companies must respect cultural differences while operating globally.

C. Cross-Cultural Challenges

- Different cultures have different ways of communicating and conducting business.
- Cultural differences can create misunderstandings.
- Businesses need cultural awareness and adaptability to manage international operations.

D. Differences in Ethical Standards

- One of the major challenges occurs when two different ethical standards meet during a business transaction.
- Different countries may follow different moral values and business practices.
- International businesses must understand and manage these differences.

### Economic Order

A new economic order is based on comparative cultural advantages.
- Many countries have cheap labour, but only a few have become economic powerhouses.
- Different countries develop competitive advantages through their culture, management practices and business environment.
Examples:
1. Japanese Quality
2. Korean Manufacturing
3. Indian Information Technology (IT)
4. Chinese Entrepreneurship
5. Rule-Based Cultures

#### Japanese Quality
Japanese business culture focuses on quality, teamwork and continuous improvement.

Key Features:
- Group-Oriented: Importance is given to teamwork and collective goals.
- Long-Time Horizon: Focus on long-term growth rather than short-term benefits.
- Group Harmony: Maintaining cooperation and harmony within the organisation.
- Continuous Improvement: Regular improvement in products, processes and working methods.
Superior Operations Management

Japanese organisations are known for efficient operations management.
- JIT (Just-in-Time) Inventory Management: Maintaining inventory according to actual requirements to reduce unnecessary stock.
- Kanban System: A visual management system used to control workflow and inventory.
  - Minimises rework and waste.
  - Maximises flexibility.

3. Korean Manufacturing
- Strong relationship between leading industrial families and the government.
- Close cooperation between business groups and government institutions.
- This relationship has contributed to industrial growth and manufacturing development.

3. Indian Information Technology (IT)
- India has developed a strong position in the global IT industry.
- IT services and technological expertise are important contributors to its economic development.

3. Chinese Entrepreneurship
- Entrepreneurship plays an important role in China's economic development.
- Business expansion and entrepreneurial activities contribute to economic growth.

3. Rule-Based Cultures
- These cultures emphasise rules, regulations and established procedures.
- Business decisions and activities are guided by formal standards and institutional frameworks.
